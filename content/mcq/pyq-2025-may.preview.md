## 1. Antigen-presenting cells (APCs) include:
*Basic Sciences → Immuno & Micro · paper Q1*

- **A. Macrophage** — ✅ True  
  Macrophages are professional APCs. They phagocytose antigen and present it on MHC class II to CD4+ T cells.
- **B. T cell** — ❌ False  
  T cells recognise antigen that other cells present. They are not professional APCs.
- **C. B cell** — ✅ True  
  B cells are professional APCs. They take up antigen through the B-cell receptor and present it on MHC II to helper T cells.
- **D. NK cell** — ❌ False  
  NK cells are innate killer lymphocytes. They kill cells that lack MHC I and do not present antigen.

**Exam pearl:** The 3 professional APCs: dendritic cells (the most potent), macrophages and B cells.

## 2. Findings in 'some dehydration' (IMCI):
*Pediatrics → IMCI, Critical Care & Emergencies · paper Q3*

- **A. Sunken eyes** — ✅ True  
  Sunken eyes are one of the IMCI signs. Two of the signs together classify as some dehydration.
- **B. Skin pinch goes back very slowly** — ❌ False  
  'Very slowly' (more than 2 seconds) is a sign of severe dehydration. In some dehydration the skin pinch goes back slowly.
- **C. Thirsty, drinks eagerly** — ✅ True  
  Drinking eagerly / thirsty is a sign of some dehydration. In severe dehydration the child drinks poorly or is unable to drink.
- **D. Unconscious** — ❌ False  
  A lethargic or unconscious child falls under severe dehydration. In some dehydration the child is restless and irritable.

**Exam pearl:** Some dehydration = 2 of: restless/irritable, sunken eyes, drinks eagerly, skin pinch goes back slowly. Treat with Plan B (ORS 75 ml/kg over 4 hours).

## 3. Characteristics of tuberculous meningitis:
*Medicine → Neurology · paper Q4*

- **A. Cobweb coagulum in CSF** — ✅ True  
  A fine cobweb (pellicle) clot forms when TB CSF is left standing, because of its high protein (fibrinogen).
- **B. Increased CSF pressure** — ✅ True  
  Opening pressure is raised, often because of basal exudates causing communicating hydrocephalus.
- **C. Decreased CSF protein** — ❌ False  
  Protein is markedly raised (often 1–5 g/L), and very high in spinal block.
- **D. Increased CSF glucose** — ❌ False  
  Glucose is low (CSF:blood ratio below 0.5), as in bacterial meningitis.

**Exam pearl:** TB CSF: clear/cobweb, lymphocytes, ↑↑ protein, ↓ glucose, ADA raised. Viral CSF has normal glucose.

## 4. Colles' fracture:
*Specialized Surgery → Orthopedics & Neurosurgery · paper Q5*

- **A. Common in younger age** — ❌ False  
  It typically occurs in elderly, post-menopausal (osteoporotic) women after a fall on the outstretched hand. Young adults get high-energy distal radius fractures instead.
- **B. Dinner-fork deformity** — ✅ True  
  Dorsal displacement of the distal fragment gives the classic dinner-fork deformity on lateral view.
- **C. Dorsal angulation** — ✅ True  
  The distal radius fragment is dorsally angulated/tilted, radially deviated and impacted, within 2.5 cm of the wrist.
- **D. Sudeck's atrophy** — ✅ True  
  Sudeck's atrophy (complex regional pain syndrome) is a recognised late complication of Colles' fracture.

**Exam pearl:** Colles complications: malunion (most common), stiffness, Sudeck's atrophy, carpal tunnel syndrome, delayed rupture of extensor pollicis longus.

## 5. Uses of the partograph:
*Obstetrics → Normal Labour, Episiotomy & Induction · paper Q8*

- **A. Recording cervical dilatation** — ✅ True  
  Cervical dilatation is plotted against time with the alert and action lines. It is the core of the partograph.
- **B. Monitoring fetal heart rate** — ✅ True  
  Fetal condition (FHR, liquor, moulding) is charted, usually every 30 minutes.
- **C. Monitoring uterine contractions** — ✅ True  
  Frequency and duration of contractions per 10 minutes are recorded.
- **D. Early detection of obstructed labour** — ✅ True  
  Crossing the alert/action line shows prolonged labour early, which helps prevent obstructed labour.

**Exam pearl:** WHO partograph starts in the active phase (cervix 4 cm). Action line is 4 hours to the right of the alert line.

## 6. Exudative ascites is found in:
*Medicine → Hepatology · paper Q9*

- **A. Chronic liver disease** — ❌ False  
  Cirrhosis causes a transudate (low protein, SAAG ≥ 1.1 g/dL) from portal hypertension and hypoalbuminaemia.
- **B. Congestive cardiac failure** — ❌ False  
  Classically a transudate (high SAAG). Protein may be mildly raised, but for exams count CCF as transudative.
- **C. Malignancy** — ✅ True  
  Peritoneal carcinomatosis gives a protein-rich exudate, often blood-stained, with malignant cells.
- **D. Tuberculosis** — ✅ True  
  TB peritonitis gives an exudate: high protein, lymphocytes and raised ADA.

**Exam pearl:** Exudate (protein > 25 g/L): malignancy, TB, pancreatitis. Transudate: cirrhosis, CCF, nephrotic syndrome. SAAG ≥ 1.1 g/dL means portal hypertension.

## 7. Complications of chronic otitis media:
*General Surgery → Ear · paper Q13*

- **A. Facial nerve palsy** — ✅ True  
  Cholesteatoma can erode the fallopian canal and cause lower motor neuron facial palsy (an intratemporal complication).
- **B. Cholesteatoma formation** — ✅ True  
  Attico-antral (unsafe) CSOM is associated with cholesteatoma, which erodes bone.
- **C. Sensorineural hearing loss** — ✅ True  
  Toxins spreading through the round window, or labyrinthitis/fistula, can cause SNHL on top of the conductive loss.
- **D. Cerebral venous sinus thrombosis** — ✅ True  
  Lateral (sigmoid) sinus thrombosis is a classic intracranial complication of unsafe CSOM.

**Exam pearl:** Intracranial complications of CSOM: meningitis (most common), brain abscess (temporal lobe / cerebellum), lateral sinus thrombosis, extradural abscess.

## 8. Drugs causing gynaecomastia:
*Basic Sciences → Pharmacology · paper Q14*

- **A. Isoniazid** — ✅ True  
  Isoniazid is a recognised (less common) cause of gynaecomastia.
- **B. Digoxin** — ✅ True  
  Digoxin has an oestrogen-like structure and can cause gynaecomastia.
- **C. Spironolactone** — ✅ True  
  Spironolactone is the classic cause: it blocks androgen receptors and testosterone synthesis. Eplerenone avoids it.
- **D. Propranolol** — ❌ False  
  Beta-blockers are not associated with gynaecomastia.

**Exam pearl:** Mnemonic 'DISCO': Digoxin, Isoniazid, Spironolactone, Cimetidine, Oestrogens. Add ketoconazole, GnRH analogues and finasteride.

## 9. Secondary hyperthyroidism:
*Medicine → Endocrinology · paper Q16*

- **A. Increased T3** — ✅ True  
  Excess TSH drives the thyroid, so T3 rises.
- **B. Increased T4** — ✅ True  
  T4 also rises because of TSH stimulation.
- **C. Increased TSH** — ✅ True  
  The cause is a TSH-secreting pituitary adenoma, so TSH is high or inappropriately normal along with high T3/T4.
- **D. Pathology lies in the thyroid gland** — ❌ False  
  The pathology is in the pituitary. Disease of the thyroid gland itself (Graves', toxic nodule) is primary hyperthyroidism, with suppressed TSH.

**Exam pearl:** Primary hyperthyroidism: ↑T3/T4, ↓TSH. Secondary: ↑T3/T4 with ↑ or normal TSH.

## 10. Irreversible cell injury:
*Basic Sciences → Pathology & Genetics · paper Q22*

- **A. Apoptosis** — ✅ True  
  Apoptosis is programmed cell death. Once it starts, the cell is lost, so it is an end point of irreversible injury.
- **B. Necrosis** — ✅ True  
  Necrosis is the classic result of irreversible injury: membrane damage, nuclear changes (pyknosis, karyorrhexis, karyolysis) and inflammation.
- **C. Metaplasia** — ❌ False  
  Metaplasia is a reversible adaptation in which one adult cell type is replaced by another (e.g. Barrett's oesophagus).
- **D. Hyperplasia** — ❌ False  
  Hyperplasia is a reversible adaptation: an increase in cell number.

**Exam pearl:** Hallmarks of irreversibility: inability to reverse mitochondrial dysfunction and severe membrane damage. Reversible signs: cell swelling and fatty change.
